Prototype for coordinated vacancy response

Make Albany’s vacant homes visible—and easier to activate.

Albany is facing a serious mismatch: thousands of homes sit “indefinitely vacant” while the city still has an effective shortage of housing. Many vacancies are tied up by repairs, legal issues, or strategic holding—so they stay scattered, hard to track, and slow to resolve. Our prototype is a centralized map that helps residents, the Albany County Land Bank, nonprofits, buyers, and rehabbers see the same inventory and take action.

What this map is for

The fastest way to unlock housing isn’t always new construction—it’s getting existing homes unstuck. This prototype focuses on the “last mile” problem: turning scattered vacancy signals into coordinated action.

  1. Residents can report vacancies and visible conditions.
  2. Land Bank & City can triage and track status (reported → in process → activated).
  3. Nonprofits can spot clusters for rehab programs and grant planning.
  4. Rehabbers/Buyers can find opportunities and signal interest.

Why a centralized hub matters

Even when vacancy is “known,” it’s often fragmented across spreadsheets, inspections, community reports, tax/foreclosure processes, and separate organizations. A shared map creates a single “source of truth” for coordinating next steps—so the same property isn’t ignored by everyone or worked on by no one.

This aligns with the Housing Audit’s emphasis on reinvestment and the importance of partners like the Albany County Land Bank to return vacant and tax-delinquent properties to productive use.

What you can do on the map (MVP)

• Browse and filter properties by type/status
• Click a pin to view details in a side drawer
• (Next) Submit “I’m interested” to create a lead
• (Next) Report a vacant property (address + pin + notes)

In the demo, we’ll show how reported properties can move through statuses over time to track progress transparently.
